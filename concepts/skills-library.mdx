Harvey's sales intelligence isn't baked into a model or stored in a vector database — it lives in plain Markdown files under `skills/`. Before an agent runs, the skills it needs are read from disk and concatenated straight into its system prompt. Change a file, and the next heartbeat behaves differently. No restart, no code change, no retraining. The skills directory is the main lever you have over how Harvey sells.

<Tip>
To change how Harvey writes emails, open `skills/email_frameworks.md` in any text editor, make your edit, and save. The change takes effect on the next heartbeat cycle — no restart required.
</Tip>

## What ships

Harvey ships with nine skill files covering every stage of the sales process, plus two more generated by `harvey train`.

<Accordion title="email_frameworks.md — 301 lines, the most important file in the repo">
Opens with the only rule that matters: *write like a real person sending a real email.* Contains:

- A **hard ban list** on AI tells — no em dashes, no "I hope this finds you well," no "I wanted to reach out," no "circling back," no bullet points in cold email
- Compliance rules (CAN-SPAM, GDPR) that override every style rule beneath them
- 2026 reply-rate data on length, cadence, link tracking, and segment size — including why tracking pixels cost 8–12% inbox placement
- Five email frameworks with selection rules for when each one fits:

| Framework | When to use |
|-----------|------------|
| **AIDA** (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action) | When you have a strong hook and a clear offer to build toward |
| **PAS** (Problem, Agitation, Solution) | When the prospect's pain is well-understood and visceral |
| **BAB** (Before, After, Bridge) | When the transformation is concrete and demonstrable |
| **QVC** (Question, Value, CTA) | For follow-ups — starts a new thread with a direct question |
| **3Ps** (Problem, Proof, Proposal) | When you have a real proof point to anchor on |

The 3-email sequence structure is also here: Email 1 as an observation with one question (under 75 words), Email 2 as a different angle with a concrete proof point (under 75 words), Email 3 as a clean breakup (under 40 words).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="signal_playbook.md — turning a signal into the first line">
How to turn a detected signal into the opening of an email. Covers:

- The core rule: the signal is the *reason you're emailing today* — say it plainly as an observation, never dress it up
- Signal-to-angle map for hiring signals, tech-stack signals, funding signals, and new-domain signals
- Which signal wins when a company has several: **hiring > funding > tech-gap > tech-present > industry problem > nothing**. Use exactly one signal as the hook — stacking two reads as a dossier
- How to follow up on a signal in Email 2 without repeating Email 1's hook
- What to do when you have no real signal (fall back to a genuine role/industry problem, stated as an observation)

Reply rate data in the file: signal-grounded emails get **5–18%** replies vs 1–3% for generic ICP-matched outreach.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="objection_handling.md — the LAARC loop and four objection families">
The operating philosophy: you are not "overcoming" an objection, you are finding out what the actual concern is. Contains:

- **LAARC loop**: Listen → Acknowledge → Assess → Respond → Confirm — with the instruction to read the full reply and identify the *actual* objection, not just the surface words
- **Four objection families**: Budget ("it's too expensive"), Authority ("I'm not the right person"), Need ("we already have a solution"), Timing ("not right now") — each with worked responses
- **Diagnosis before response**: reflex brush-off vs condition vs smokescreen vs genuine objection — the reply length is the signal (long reply = engagement, respond substantively; one-liner = keep your response shorter than theirs)
- Advanced techniques: the Isolate & Reverse, Feel-Felt-Found, Cost of Inaction, The Takeaway
- Hard ethical guardrails: opt-outs are absolute (not an objection to handle), never fabricate, answer truthfully if asked about automation, one "no" is enough
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="lead_qualification.md — BANT to ICP scoring to MEDDIC">
A three-tier qualification system:

1. **BANT** for initial screening: Budget (can they afford it?), Authority (is this a decision-maker?), Need (do they have the problem?), Timeline (are they likely to buy soon?)
2. **ICP scoring 1–10** for prioritization — four criteria (title match 0–3, company fit 0–3, engagement signals 0–2, reachability 0–2) producing priority tiers: 8–10 immediate personalized outreach, 5–7 batch campaigns, 3–4 only if pipeline needs volume, 1–2 skip
3. **MEDDIC** for complex deals: Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Identify Pain, Champion

Also includes **explicit disqualification criteria** — which matter more than qualification ones. Stop pursuing if: below minimum team size, unrelated industry, no discoverable contact, deeply entrenched competitor without switching signals, already opted out.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="sales_methodology.md — the ABC loop and operating philosophy">
Harvey's operating philosophy: the ABC loop (Always Be Closing) mapped to the sales cycle:

```text
Prospect → Qualify → Outreach → Engage → Close → Learn → Repeat
```

Covers:
- Multi-channel orchestration: which channel to use for which seniority level, how to sequence across email and LinkedIn, rules for switching channels after a reply
- Conversation flow from reply to meeting: propose 2–3 specific time slots, include timezone, keep each reply under 80 words, answer their question AND propose a meeting in the same reply
- **COSTAR framework** for tone calibration: Context, Objective, Style, Tone, Audience, Response format
- Harvey's voice: writes like a smart colleague, uses contractions, asks questions instead of making statements, never "just following up"
- Ten ethical guidelines that override every other instruction — including bot disclosure, opt-out permanence, and CAN-SPAM compliance
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="offer_strategy.md — the offer ladder and the cold-email rule">
The governing rule: **never lead with an offer, never pitch in a cold email.** First contact exists to start a conversation. Offers come out only after genuine interest. Contains:

- **The offer ladder** matched to engagement level: Curious → answer their question + soft CTA; Interested → relevant resource + call suggestion; Ready → specific offer with clear next step; Urgent → move fast
- Four closing mechanics: calendar link, suggest specific times, ask for their preference, next step without a call — each with decision rules for when to use which
- Offer construction framework: what they get, what it costs, what they risk, what happens next
- Sequence timing: Email 1 no offer, Email 2 soft mention only if relevant, Email 3 break-up with no hard pitch
- Language rules: what to say ("worth a quick conversation?") and what never to say ("book a demo," "let me know if you're interested")
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="prospecting_tactics.md — search operators and trigger events">
Finding companies and contacts without paying for Apollo, ZoomInfo, or Clearbit:

- **Google dorking** operators for LinkedIn profiles, company team pages, and email patterns
- **LinkedIn search** without Sales Navigator: people search, "People Also Viewed," company pages, group browsing
- **Company website scraping**: team/about pages, job postings, tech stack detection
- **Email pattern discovery**: nine common formats in prevalence order, four free verification methods, catch-all domain handling
- **Trigger events**: the eight highest-value timing signals (new hire, recent funding, new product launch, job posting, competitor usage, company expansion, leadership change, tech stack change) — and where to find them for free
- **Referral mining**: what to do when a prospect says "I'm not the right person"
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="account_navigation.md — companies vs contacts, multi-threading rules">
How to work one account with multiple contacts:

- Company profile requirements (name, domain, website, description, industry, size, location, source URL)
- Contact profile requirements — required fields vs optional, and the data quality rule: never fabricate, email patterns only valid after SMTP verification
- **Entry point matrix**: who to contact first based on deal size (small purchase → end user/manager; mid-market → Director/VP; enterprise → VP/C-suite)
- **Multi-threading rules**: start with one person, wait at least 5 business days before emailing a second contact at the same company, share context across contacts, move up or down based on response
- When to stop: 3 contacts emailed with no response, any contact explicitly declines, company doesn't match ICP on further research
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="linkedin_outreach.md — connection sequences and rate limits">
Harvey's LinkedIn playbook. Opens with the risk section it must open with.

<Warning>
**Automating LinkedIn violates LinkedIn's Terms of Service.** The operator was warned during setup. An automated account can be restricted or banned. The rate limits in this file are ceilings, not targets. Never use a fake profile or fake identity. If someone asks whether messages are automated, answer truthfully, immediately.
</Warning>

The skill covers:
- Connection request strategy: no-note (34.3% acceptance) vs personalized note (32.3%) — use notes only when you have something genuinely relevant to say
- Post-connection messaging sequence: no pitch on Day 0, genuine question on Day 1–2, soft product mention only if there's a natural opening on Day 4–5, stop after 2 messages with no reply
- Profile warm-up sequence over 2–3 weeks to increase acceptance rates by up to 25%
- Safe rate limits: max 20 connection requests/day, max 10 messages/day to new connections, max 50 profile views/day, max 100 connection requests/week, 3–8 second delays between actions
</Accordion>

## Generated skills from `harvey train`

Two additional skills are generated by running `harvey train <url>` against your product's website. They are gitignored — your positioning and pricing shouldn't land in a public repo by accident.

| File | Contents |
|------|----------|
| `product_knowledge.md` | What you sell, the key benefits, pricing, use cases, the pain you solve, buying triggers. Loaded by every agent. |
| `competitive_intel.md` | Battle cards per competitor, differentiation angles, migration paths. Loaded by Writer and Handler. |

Run `harvey train https://your-company.com` to generate or update them. Re-running updates the product knowledge and keeps your other settings. You can also pass `--provider codex --model gpt-5.5` to use a ChatGPT plan instead.

## Adding a new skill

The whole process is two steps:

```bash
# 1. Write the file
echo "# My Custom Skill\n\n..." > skills/my_skill.md

# 2. Add its name to skill_map in harvey/brain.py
#    e.g. add "my_skill" to the "writer" list
```

The next heartbeat cycle picks it up automatically. No restart, no configuration change beyond the one line in `brain.py`.

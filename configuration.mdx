Harvey is configured through two plain-text files that live at the root of the project. Neither requires a restart to take effect — the heartbeat loop reads config on every cycle. `.env` holds credentials (API keys, OAuth secrets, passwords) and is gitignored. `harvey.yaml` holds everything else: who Harvey is, what it sells, who it targets, and how it behaves. Both files ship with documented examples you can edit in place.

## harvey.yaml

The main configuration file. `harvey train <url>` writes the `persona`, `product`, and `icp` sections for you by crawling your website. You can edit any section by hand at any time.

### persona

The identity Harvey uses in every email and conversation.

```yaml
persona:
  name: "Harvey"
  company: "Your Company"
  role: "Business Development"
  email: "harvey@yourcompany.com"
  linkedin: "linkedin.com/in/harvey-yourcompany"
  tone: "professional, consultative, confident"
```

<ParamField body="name" type="string" required>
  The sender name that appears in outgoing emails and LinkedIn messages.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="company" type="string" required>
  Your company name. Used in email copy and product descriptions.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="role" type="string" required>
  Harvey's job title as it appears in signatures and introductions. E.g. `"Business Development"`, `"Account Executive"`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="email" type="string" required>
  The sending email address. Must match the mailbox configured in `channels.email`. Should be on a dedicated secondary domain, not your main company domain.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="tone" type="string">
  A comma-separated description of the writing style Harvey should adopt. Passed to the Writer and Handler agents. E.g. `"direct, conversational, brief"`.
</ParamField>

### product

What Harvey is selling. The Writer and Handler agents use this section to craft personalized emails and handle objections. `harvey train <url>` generates this from your website and writes it to `harvey.local.yaml`.

```yaml
product:
  name: "Your Product"
  description: "Describe what your product does and who it's for"
  pricing: "Starting at $X/mo"
  key_benefits:
    - "Benefit 1"
    - "Benefit 2"
    - "Benefit 3"
  objection_responses:
    "too expensive": "I understand budget is important. Most of our clients see ROI within 30 days..."
    "already have a solution": "That makes sense. Curious — what's the one thing you wish it did better?"
    "not interested": "No worries at all. Mind if I ask what your current approach looks like?"
  offer:
    primary: ""                    # Main offer (e.g., "Monthly subscription starting at $99/mo")
    entry: ""                      # Low-commitment entry (e.g., "14-day free trial", "Free audit")
    goal: "book_call"              # book_call | start_trial | get_reply
    booking_method: "calendar_link" # calendar_link | suggest_times | ask_preference
    booking_url: ""                # Calendar link URL (when using calendar_link)
    meeting_duration: "15 minutes"
    meeting_owner: ""              # Name or role of whoever takes the meeting
```

<ParamField body="offer.goal" type="string">
  The conversion goal for cold outreach. `book_call` is the default — Harvey's emails guide prospects toward scheduling a call. Other options: `start_trial`, `get_reply`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="offer.booking_method" type="string">
  How Harvey proposes a meeting time. `calendar_link` drops the `booking_url` into the email; `suggest_times` has Harvey propose two or three slots; `ask_preference` asks the prospect how they'd like to connect.
</ParamField>

### icp

Your Ideal Customer Profile. Used by Scout to score prospects and by the cohort builder in the dashboard.

```yaml
icp:
  industries:
    - "SaaS"
    - "Marketing Agency"
  company_size: "10-200 employees"
  titles:
    - "VP Marketing"
    - "Head of Growth"
    - "CMO"
    - "Director of Marketing"
  geography:
    - "United States"
  hiring_signals: []
  # geo_coordinates is used for radius-based discovery queries
  # geo_coordinates:
  #   - lat: 45.5051
  #     lon: -122.6750
  #     radius_km: 50
  #     label: "Portland, OR"
```

<ParamField body="industries" type="string[]" required>
  Industries Harvey should target. Used to filter discovery results and weight Scout's ICP scores.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="titles" type="string[]" required>
  Job titles of your target decision-makers. Harvey uses these to identify the right contact at each company.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="company_size" type="string">
  A human-readable range like `"10-200 employees"`. Guides Scout's scoring and the Writer's framing.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="geography" type="string[]">
  The geographic markets to prospect. Used to seed discovery queries. E.g. `["United States"]`, `["Portland, OR", "Seattle, WA"]`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="hiring_signals" type="string[]">
  Job roles that signal a company is in-market right now. A company hiring a "Head of Growth" is likely shopping for growth tooling. Harvey scans careers pages and job boards for these. Empty means fall back to `titles`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="geo_coordinates" type="object[]">
  Explicit latitude/longitude coordinates for radius-based discovery queries. When set, Harvey can run `radius_km`-radius searches from each point instead of relying on city-name geocoding. Geocoded city names are cached permanently; `geo_coordinates` skips that step entirely.
</ParamField>

### channels.email

Controls how Harvey sends email and manages the outbox. Set `channels.email.provider` to match the credentials you filled in `.env`.

```yaml
channels:
  email:
    enabled: true
    provider: "gmail"          # gmail | microsoft | smtp | instantly
    max_daily_sends: 50
    require_approval: true
    send_to_risky: false
    max_bounce_rate: 0.05
    warmup:
      start_date: ""           # YYYY-MM-DD; blank = no ramp
      min_days: 14
      start_sends: 5
      daily_increase: 3
      dkim_selector: ""        # only if your provider uses a non-standard one
```

<ParamField body="provider" type="string" required>
  The mail provider Harvey uses to send and poll for replies. Options: `gmail`, `microsoft`, `smtp`, `instantly`. Must match the credentials set in `.env`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="max_daily_sends" type="integer">
  Hard ceiling on outgoing emails per day. Default `50`. This is a ceiling, not a target — Harvey ramps from much lower during warmup. During warmup, the effective limit is computed from `warmup.start_sends` and `warmup.daily_increase` regardless of this value.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="require_approval" type="boolean">
  When `true` (default), every outgoing email waits in the Outbox for your approval before sending. Set to `false` for full autopilot. When using the `instantly` provider, `true` holds campaign deployment rather than individual emails.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="send_to_risky" type="boolean">
  When `false` (default, safest), Harvey skips catch-all domains. When `true`, it sends to addresses marked `risky` (catch-all domain) as well as `verified` ones. Addresses marked `guess` or `invalid` are never sent to regardless of this setting.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="max_bounce_rate" type="number">
  The bounce rate threshold at which Harvey trips its sending kill switch. Default `0.05` (5%). Above this rate, all outbound stops until you investigate and resume with `harvey sending resume`.
</ParamField>

#### warmup sub-object

Use `channels.email.warmup` when you're sending from a new domain. Harvey enforces a cold-email freeze for `min_days`, then ramps the daily send cap from `start_sends` by `daily_increase` each day until it reaches `max_daily_sends`. Reply emails are never held by warmup — only cold outreach.

<ParamField body="warmup.start_date" type="string">
  The date your warmup service started, in `YYYY-MM-DD` format. Leave blank to skip the warmup ramp entirely (only appropriate for an already-warmed domain). Check readiness at any time with `harvey warmup`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="warmup.min_days" type="integer">
  Days after `start_date` during which no cold email is sent. Default `14`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="warmup.start_sends" type="integer">
  The cold-email cap on the first day after the warmup freeze ends. Default `5`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="warmup.daily_increase" type="integer">
  How many emails to add to the daily cap each day after warmup ends. Default `3`. At this rate, starting at 5 and adding 3/day, you reach 50/day after ~15 days.
</ParamField>

### channels.linkedin

LinkedIn outreach is **off by default** and requires an explicit opt-in. Browser automation violates LinkedIn's Terms of Service — use an account you can afford to lose.

```yaml
channels:
  linkedin:
    enabled: false             # must be true AND credentials set in .env
    max_daily_connections: 20
    max_daily_messages: 10
```

<ParamField body="enabled" type="boolean">
  Must be explicitly set to `true` to activate LinkedIn. Credentials alone in `.env` do not enable it.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="max_daily_connections" type="integer">
  Maximum connection requests Harvey sends per day. Default `20`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="max_daily_messages" type="integer">
  Maximum LinkedIn messages Harvey sends per day to existing connections. Default `10`.
</ParamField>

### usage

Controls how much of your subscription Harvey is allowed to use and when it runs.

```yaml
usage:
  max_daily_claude_percent: 80
  heartbeat_interval_minutes: 15
  quiet_hours:
    start: "22:00"
    end: "07:00"
    timezone: "America/New_York"
```

<ParamField body="max_daily_claude_percent" type="integer">
  The percentage of your subscription's daily quota Harvey may consume. The remaining headroom is left for your own Claude or Codex work. Default `80`. Despite the name, this setting applies to whichever provider `brain.provider` is set to — the name predates Codex support. If the quota cannot be read, Harvey falls back to its own daily call budget.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="heartbeat_interval_minutes" type="integer">
  How long Harvey sleeps between cycles. Default `15` minutes. Shorter intervals are more responsive; longer intervals are more conservative with your subscription quota.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="quiet_hours.start" type="string">
  Time (HH:MM, 24-hour) when Harvey stops sending and prospecting. Default `"22:00"`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="quiet_hours.end" type="string">
  Time (HH:MM, 24-hour) when Harvey resumes. Default `"07:00"`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="quiet_hours.timezone" type="string">
  IANA timezone for quiet hours. Default `"America/New_York"`. E.g. `"America/Los_Angeles"`, `"Europe/London"`.
</ParamField>

### brain *(optional)*

Controls which AI provider and model Harvey thinks with. Claude Code is the default and requires no `brain` section at all.

```yaml
brain:
  provider: codex              # claude (default) | codex
  model: gpt-5.5               # exact model ID from your account's catalog
  reasoning_effort: ""         # codex only: minimal | low | medium | high | xhigh
```

<ParamField body="provider" type="string">
  Which subscription-backed CLI to use. `claude` (default) uses Claude Code. `codex` uses the Codex CLI on a ChatGPT plan. It's one or the other — Harvey never falls back from one to the other.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="model" type="string">
  The exact model ID to use. For Codex, this can be any model available in your account's catalog including Luna, Sol, and Terra models (e.g. `gpt-5.6-luna`, `gpt-6.1-sol`). Leave blank to let Harvey select the highest-priority listed model. For Claude, Harvey uses whichever model the CLI defaults to for your subscription tier.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="reasoning_effort" type="string">
  Codex only. Controls how much reasoning the model applies. Options: `minimal`, `low`, `medium`, `high`, `xhigh`. Leave blank for the model's default.
</ParamField>

<Note>
Using Codex requires the Codex CLI version `0.159.3` exactly on macOS or Linux. Harvey refuses any other version — including newer ones — because a different Codex version can introduce tools Harvey's lockdown doesn't account for. Install with:

```bash
npm install -g @openai/codex@0.159.3
codex login    # choose "Sign in with ChatGPT" — API-key logins are refused
```
</Note>

### writer *(optional)*

```yaml
writer:
  humanizer: true
```

<ParamField body="humanizer" type="boolean">
  When `true`, adds one extra editing pass per email batch using `~/.claude/skills/humanizer/SKILL.md` if that file exists. Intended for users who have written their own humanizer skill. Default: not set (no extra pass).
</ParamField>

### compliance

Controls CAN-SPAM footer content and optional per-recipient compliance checking via [Precleared](https://precleared.dev).

```yaml
compliance:
  postal_address: ""
  opt_out_line: 'Not interested? Reply "no thanks" and I won''t email again.'
  precleared:
    enabled: false
    sender_country: ""          # GB or US: where your business is based
    address_source: "unknown"   # published | bought | scraped | given | unknown
    prospect_country: ""        # optional: e.g. US if all your prospects are in one country
```

<Warning>
**CAN-SPAM requires a postal address on every cold email.** Set `compliance.postal_address` to a street address, PO box, or virtual mailbox where you receive mail before your first campaign. Until it's set, cold email still sends — but Harvey warns you on `harvey run`, `harvey status`, and the Outbox tab on every cycle.
</Warning>

<ParamField body="postal_address" type="string">
  Your physical or virtual mailing address, appended to the footer of every cold email. Required by CAN-SPAM. E.g. `"123 Main St, Portland OR 97201"`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="opt_out_line" type="string">
  The opt-out sentence Harvey appends to every cold email. Must ask for a reply Harvey recognises as an opt-out (`"no thanks"`, `"unsubscribe"`, `"STOP"`, `"remove me"`, `"opt out"`). Must not contain a link or email address. Any reply matching that wording is treated as an immediate and permanent opt-out.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="precleared.enabled" type="boolean">
  When `true`, Harvey checks each new recipient with Precleared before the first cold email. Anything other than `ok_to_email` holds the email in the Outbox with the reason shown. One check per address, cached for 30 days. Applies to the `gmail`, `microsoft`, and `smtp` providers.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="precleared.sender_country" type="string">
  Two-letter ISO country code for where your business is based. Used by Precleared to apply the right legal framework. E.g. `"US"`, `"GB"`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="precleared.address_source" type="string">
  How you obtained each prospect's email address. Options: `published`, `bought`, `scraped`, `given`, `unknown`. Affects Precleared's analysis under GDPR and CASL.
</ParamField>

## .env variable reference

Credentials only — never put these in `harvey.yaml`. The `.env` file is gitignored. Copy `.env.example` to get started.

| Variable | Required | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `GMAIL_CLIENT_ID` | If using Gmail | OAuth 2.0 client ID from Google Cloud Console → Credentials → Desktop app |
| `GMAIL_CLIENT_SECRET` | If using Gmail | OAuth 2.0 client secret for the same credential |
| `M365_CLIENT_ID` | If using Microsoft 365 | Application (client) ID from Entra → App registrations |
| `M365_TENANT_ID` | If using Microsoft 365 | Directory (tenant) ID for your organization |
| `SMTP_HOST` | If using SMTP | Your outbound SMTP server hostname |
| `SMTP_PORT` | If using SMTP | SMTP port, typically `587` (STARTTLS) |
| `SMTP_USERNAME` | If using SMTP | SMTP login username (usually your full email address) |
| `SMTP_PASSWORD` | If using SMTP | SMTP password or app-specific password |
| `REOON_API_KEY` | Strongly recommended | Email verification — 600 checks/month free, renews monthly. Get it at [reoon.com](https://reoon.com/email-verifier) |
| `ZEROBOUNCE_API_KEY` | Optional | Backup email verifier — 100 free/month. Better for Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace catch-all domains. |
| `HUNTER_API_KEY` | Optional | Email pattern discovery — 25 domain searches/month free. Lets Harvey verify one address per company instead of guessing. |
| `DATAFORSEO_LOGIN` | Optional | DataForSEO login email. Cheapest paid discovery source (~$0.37/1,000 businesses). $1 free credit on signup. |
| `DATAFORSEO_PASSWORD` | Optional | DataForSEO API password (auto-generated in the dashboard — not your account password) |
| `SERPER_API_KEY` | Optional | Serper.dev Google Search API — 2,500 queries free, no card required. Used for discovery and Scout searches. |
| `PRECLEARED_API_KEY` | Optional | Per-recipient compliance checking — 10 checks/day free, 25/day with a free key. Required only when `compliance.precleared.enabled: true`. |
| `LINKEDIN_EMAIL` | Optional | LinkedIn account email for browser-automation prospecting. See LinkedIn ToS warning. |
| `LINKEDIN_PASSWORD` | Optional | LinkedIn account password for browser-automation prospecting. |
| `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` | Optional | Enables JS-rendered crawling of your own site during `harvey train`. |
| `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` | Optional | Cloudflare API token with "Browser Rendering: Edit" permission. |

## harvey.local.yaml

Harvey loads `harvey.local.yaml` in addition to `harvey.yaml` when it exists, and **values in `harvey.local.yaml` always win**. It is gitignored.

<Note>
`harvey.local.yaml` is the recommended place for real product configuration. A fork can carry your actual positioning and pricing in `harvey.local.yaml` while the tracked `harvey.yaml` stays a template — nobody publishes their pricing and ICP by accident. The `harvey train` command writes here, not to `harvey.yaml`.
</Note>

The override applies at the key level within each section. You don't need to repeat the whole file — only the keys you want to change:

```yaml
# harvey.local.yaml — only your overrides
persona:
  email: "hey@acme.io"
  tone: "direct, brief, no buzzwords"

product:
  name: "Acme Widget"
  pricing: "Starts at $199/mo"

compliance:
  postal_address: "456 Oak Ave, Suite 100, Austin TX 78701"
```

When Harvey reads config, it deep-merges `harvey.yaml` and `harvey.local.yaml`, with `harvey.local.yaml` taking precedence for every key that appears in both. Keys present only in `harvey.yaml` are used as-is.
